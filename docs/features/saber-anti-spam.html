Saber anti-spam

A bind or script can turn a saber on and off every server frame, or loop weapon switches onto it, and every ignition and shutdown plays. This gives each player a small budget, on your screen only: a burst of toggles plays as usual, then one toggle sounds every half second for as long as the spam goes on.

cg_saberAntiSpam is archived and defaults to on. Four toggles in a row play as usual, then one every half second, and the budget refills at that pace, so quick toggling by hand is never cut.

How to use

/cg_saberAntiSpam 1    // default — hold each player's saber toggle spam
/cg_saberAntiSpam 0    // every ignition and shutdown plays

Client-side, takes effect immediately, saved to the config. Also in Setup → Sound → Saber Anti-Spam (main menu and in game).

Toggles from one playerWhat you hear
Up to 4 back to backEvery one
Kept up past thatOne every 500 ms
After 2 s of quietThe full 4 again

Only the audio is suppressed — the saber still lights and goes out as the server says.

Why not a debounce per sound

PR #74 kept one 800 ms timer per sound. Review found two problems with it:

  • Normal quick toggling lost sounds. On, off, on within 800 ms skipped the second ignition.
  • Duals lost a hilt. Each hilt's sound had its own timer, so one hilt of a toggle could play while the other was held back.

The budget fixes both. A burst plays untouched, and every sound of one toggle — both hilts of a dual or a staff — gets the same answer, so a toggle is heard whole or not at all, and every toggle pays for itself however it arrives (see Known limits).

Known limits

  • The numbers are fixed. Four and 500 ms, no cvar for either. Easy to change in cg_event.c if testing says otherwise.
  • How a toggle is told apart. A weapon switch, the staff put-away and EV_SABER_UNHOLSTER voice a whole toggle in one call, and each call pays. The server's toggle is one G_Sound per hilt, each its own temp entity, and several toggles can arrive in one snapshot (a server sending snapshots slower than it runs, a client's rate), or several snapshots in one frame. A hilt's sound joins the toggle before it only from the same snapshot, the same way (on or off), and a hilt that toggle has not sounded yet; anything else is a new toggle. A hilt whose one file is used for both ways pays on every sound.
  • Owner by position. G_Sound names no owner. Two players standing within a few units with the same hilt can be confused, and the cap then lands on the nearer one.
  • Players only. NPCs aren't capped — no one binds their toggles.
  • Your view only. Other players without this client still hear the spam.

Testing notes

tests/saber-anti-spam compiles the real budget and CG_CheckPlayerG2Weapons and checks the burst and interval, the hilts of one toggle (duals, a shared sound, one file both ways), toggles arriving in one snapshot, a backlog of snapshots in one frame, a staff's late shutdown copy, the G_Sound owner, players kept apart, NPCs and the cvar, and time going back:

cmake -S tests/saber-anti-spam -B build/saber-anti-spam-check
cmake --build build/saber-anti-spam-check --config Release
ctest --test-dir build/saber-anti-spam-check -C Release --output-on-failure

In game, two clients, or a client plus a second instance on a listen server:

  • On the other client, with the saber out, mash the saber key (pressing it again toggles the blade) next to you: four sounds, then about two a second.
  • Toggle normally — on, off, on, a second or so apart: every sound plays.
  • A weapon 1; weapon 3 loop: capped the same way.
  • Dual sabers and a staff: both hilts sound together on every toggle that plays.
  • cg_saberAntiSpam 0 — the spam is back.
